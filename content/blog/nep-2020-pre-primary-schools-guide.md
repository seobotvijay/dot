NEP 2020 — India's National Education Policy, released in 2020 — is the first national policy to formally bring children aged 3 to 8 into the school system as a single "Foundational Stage." For pre-primary schools, that's not a minor footnote: it changes what a curriculum for 3–6 year-olds is expected to look like, and who owns responsibility for getting it right.

This guide covers what actually changed, in plain language, without assuming you've read the full policy document.

## The 10+2 structure is gone

For decades, Indian schooling was described as 10+2: ten years of school followed by two years of senior secondary. NEP 2020 replaces that with a 5+3+3+4 structure:

- **Foundational Stage (5 years):** 3 years of pre-primary/preschool + Grades 1–2
- **Preparatory Stage (3 years):** Grades 3–5
- **Middle Stage (3 years):** Grades 6–8
- **Secondary Stage (4 years):** Grades 9–12

The key shift for pre-primary schools is the first bracket. Ages 3–6, which used to sit outside the formal school system in most policy conversations, are now explicitly Year 1–3 of a child's schooling — grouped together with Grades 1 and 2, not treated as a separate "before school" phase.

## What NEP 2020 expects from the Foundational Stage

NEP 2020 and the related National Curriculum Framework for Foundational Stage (NCF) are specific about *how* these years should be taught, not just that they should exist. The broad expectations are:

- **Play-based and activity-based learning**, not textbook-driven instruction
- **Multiple areas of development** addressed together — language, cognitive, motor, social-emotional, and creative — rather than literacy and numeracy in isolation
- **Formal reading and writing introduced gradually**, generally not before age 5–6
- **Trained educators** for early years, recognizing that teaching 3–6 year-olds is a distinct skill from teaching older grades

For a pre-primary school, this means a curriculum built around worksheets and rote memorization doesn't just feel outdated — it's misaligned with the stated national framework your school may eventually be evaluated against.

## What this means for your curriculum, in practice

If you're evaluating (or building) a pre-primary curriculum with NEP 2020 in mind, three things matter most:

1. **Coverage across multiple developmental areas**, not just early literacy and numeracy. A curriculum organized around a handful of narrow subjects won't reflect the whole-child approach NEP calls for.
2. **A play-based delivery model** — daily activities, not worksheets, as the primary teaching method.
3. **Structured teacher training**, since NEP's expectations only translate into classroom practice if educators are trained to deliver play-based learning, not simply handed a textbook.

## The bottom line

NEP 2020 doesn't just add new terminology to early childhood education — it puts pre-primary schooling on the same policy footing as the rest of the school system, with specific expectations about how those years should be taught. Schools that treat pre-primary as a lighter, less structured stage are increasingly out of step with where the policy — and parent expectations — are heading.
